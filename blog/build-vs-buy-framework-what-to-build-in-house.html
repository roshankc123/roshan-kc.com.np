Build vs. Buy: The Framework We Use to Decide What to Build In-House

Direct answer: We build in-house when the capability is a customer-facing differentiator or encodes our unique business rules; we buy (or adopt first-party packages) when the problem is commodity infrastructure — payments, auth primitives, email delivery — because the maintenance tax of owning commodity code is usually higher than the license or SaaS fee.

This is how we decide at MarginTop Solutions, whether the product is ours or a client’s.

Definition: A build vs buy framework is a decision model that separates differentiators (worth owning) from commodities (worth renting), then prices the long-term maintenance tax of each option.

The two-question filter

  1. Differentiator or commodity? Would a customer choose us because of this subsystem?
  2. Who feels the maintenance tax? Security patches, PCI surface, edge cases, on-call — is that our unique advantage or a distraction?

Laravel examples we actually use

  • Stripe + Cashier — billing mechanics are commodity; our pricing packages and entitlement rules are the differentiator we keep in domain code.
  • Sanctum — token/SPA auth primitives are bought; permission matrices and tenant isolation rules are built.
  • Managed email/SMS providers — delivery is bought; notification content and trigger logic are built.

Related stack choices: Laravel over Node and business logic first.

The maintenance tax nobody budgets

  • Custom auth that almost passes SOC-ish checklists until an edge case does not.
  • Homegrown payment state machines that diverge from Stripe’s realities.
  • Internal “platform” libraries with one maintainer and twelve dependents.

That tax shows up in hiring too — see what I look for when hiring. We want engineers owning product outcomes, not babysitting commodity plumbing.

Trade-offs

  • Buy too much: vendor lock-in, awkward domain fits, surprise pricing.
  • Build too much: slower roadmap, security debt, key-person risk.
  • Healthy middle: buy the rails; build the route the customer pays for.

Also pair with what NOT to build and security basics.

Key takeaways

  • Build differentiators; buy commodities.
  • Cashier/Sanctum-style packages are “buy” with room to encode unique rules on top.
  • Always price the maintenance tax, not just the first sprint estimate.
  • Revisit decisions when a commodity becomes your moat — or when your moat was never real.
