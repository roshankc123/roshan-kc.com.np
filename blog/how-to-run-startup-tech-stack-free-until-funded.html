How to Run Your Startup Tech Stack for Free Until You're Funded

Direct answer: Early-stage startups can often run cloud infrastructure near $0 for the first 6–18 months by stacking free-tier and promotional cloud credits for startups from AWS Activate, Microsoft for Startups (Azure), and Google Cloud for Startups — but only after you have a real company identity: entity, domain website, and business email. Apply to the no-investor tiers first, set billing alerts, and treat credit amounts as temporary runway, not a permanent architecture plan.

As CTO of MarginTop Solutions, I see bootstrapped teams burn cash on cloud before they have product signal. Credits do not replace engineering discipline — they buy time to validate. Below is the practical stack we recommend founders prepare before they touch a production region.

Note: Credit ceilings and eligibility change by cohort. Figures below reflect official program pages as of September 2026. Always re-check the provider’s live startup page before you apply.

What do you need before applying for free cloud credits?

Startup cloud credit programs are not “sign up with Gmail and hope.” Most rejections we see are identity failures, not product failures. Fix these before you open any application form:

  • A registered business entity — LLC, Pvt. Ltd., Inc., or local equivalent. Some programs tolerate sole proprietors; most prefer an incorporated company.
  • A live company website on your own domain — not a bare Notion page forever. Show a real product, MVP, or clear roadmap. “Coming soon” with no substance gets filtered.
  • A business email on that domain — you@yourstartup.com, not Gmail/Yahoo/Outlook consumer. This is one of the most common silent rejection reasons.
  • A one-line pitch — programs ask for a short company description. Write it once and reuse it.

Definition: Free cloud credits for startups are promotional balances issued by cloud providers to eligible early-stage companies. They offset usage of compute, storage, databases, and some AI services for a limited period (often about 12 months per grant), subject to program terms — they are not cash and usually exclude Marketplace purchases and premium support.

AWS Activate credits: Founders, Portfolio, and AI paths

According to the official AWS Activate Credits page, eligibility generally includes pre-Series B status, founding within the last 10 years, an AWS account on a paid-tier plan, and either being new to Activate or requesting a larger package than you previously received.

  • Activate Founders (self-funded): Start with $1,000 in Activate credits. Select participants may qualify for additional credits up to $5,000 total. No VC/accelerator Org ID required — apply directly if you are bootstrapped.
  • Activate Portfolio (provider-backed): Up to $200,000 with an Organization ID from an Activate Provider (accelerator, angel, VC). Older blog posts still say “$100K”; use the live AWS table.
  • AWS Credits for AI Startups: Invite-only path for startups ready to scale after Portfolio, listed as $200,000+ via account manager — not a self-serve “apply for $300K” form.

Credits are typically usable across core services such as compute, serverless, object storage, and managed databases. Do not assume Marketplace SKUs or premium support are covered — check the promotional credit terms for your offer.

AWS says you usually hear back in 5–10 business days after linking your Builder ID and account.

Microsoft for Startups: Azure credits without (and with) investors

Microsoft’s official Learn overview states that startups can unlock up to $150,000 in Startup credits over time as they show verified progress and sustained Azure usage. Investor Network–backed startups may unlock higher-value benefits earlier with a partner referral code. Entry is a single application at startups.microsoft.com; reviews are often within about three business days.

Eligibility highlights from Microsoft: software product you own, privately held for-profit, headquartered where Azure is available, under $350,000 lifetime free Azure credits, and not Series C or later. Consultancies, agencies, and crypto mining are typically excluded.

  • No-investor path: Apply without a code, create/link Azure, and start with starter Startup credits while eligibility is verified, then unlock more through program progress.
  • Investor Network path: Enter a partner referral code during apply to unlock richer benefits sooner (still capped by current program rules — treat $150K as the published ceiling, not a guarantee on day one).
  • Tooling bundle: The real bonus for many teams is adjacent tooling access (historically including GitHub and productivity perks). Confirm the live benefits list in your dashboard — bundles change.

Google Cloud for Startups: Start vs Scale vs AI

Google’s official program benefits page currently frames two primary tiers:

  • Start tier: Up to $2,000 in Google Cloud credits for one year. Aimed at digital-native startups with a working MVP and plans to seek venture funding. Founded within the last 24 months, and not yet received Google Cloud credits beyond the free trial.
  • Scale tier: Up to $200,000 (up to $350,000 for AI-first startups) structured across two years (e.g., Year 1 coverage up to $100K / $250K for AI-first, Year 2 partial coverage up to an additional $100K). Designed for VC-funded Pre-Seed/Seed (within last 5 years) or Series A (within last 12 months), founded within the last five years.

Domain hygiene matters here. Keep your company website domain, applicant email domain, and billing identity consistent. Mismatches are a frequent rejection pattern across providers — Google is especially picky about company identity signals.

Friends-and-family or pure angel funding often does not unlock Scale; Google’s footnotes call out institutional / VC-style equity (SAFE included) for Scale qualification.

How to apply for AWS, Azure, and Google startup credits step by step

  1. Register the domain and business email first. This is the single highest-leverage step across all three programs.
  2. Incorporate if you have not (even a simple LLC / local company registration).
  3. Ship a credible website — product, waitlist with substance, or roadmap that proves you are a real software company.
  4. Apply to no-investor / self-serve tiers first: AWS Activate Founders, Microsoft for Startups without a code, Google Start tier.
  5. Stack all three. They are separate programs with separate accounts and terms. Applying to all is normal.
  6. If you later join an accelerator or VC, request their AWS Org ID / Microsoft Investor Network code / Google Scale eligibility path immediately — higher tiers often require that affiliation.
  7. Set billing alerts at 50%, 75%, and 90% of credit balance (and calendar reminders near expiry). Credits usually expire on a clock; Azure terms can vary by offer.

What other free startup tools should you stack?

Cloud is only part of the $0 runway story. After credits, tighten the rest of the toolkit:

  • GitHub — often improved via Microsoft for Startups benefits; confirm current Enterprise/team offers in your portal.
  • Stripe Atlas — incorporation and banking perks for eligible founders (geo-dependent).
  • HubSpot for Startups — CRM discounts when you are still pre-revenue.
  • Notion for Startups — workspace credits/discounts for early teams.
  • Cloudflare / domain registrar startup programs — DNS, CDN, and security free tiers that pair well with a lean stack.

What are the trade-offs of building on free cloud credits?

Credits are real money in disguise — with strings.

  • Expiry forces migration anxiety. Architect for portability (containers, IaC, managed DBs you can re-home) before you are emotionally married to one vendor.
  • Not everything is covered. Marketplace, some third-party models, and support tiers may bill cash while “credits” look healthy.
  • Eligibility is discretionary. Google explicitly notes acceptance is at their discretion; Microsoft and AWS can decline incomplete profiles.
  • Multi-cloud stacking adds ops overhead. Three accounts means three IAM models, three bill dashboards, three ways to misconfigure egress.

We would reconsider multi-cloud stacking for a solo founder still pre-MVP — pick one provider, win the Founders/Start tier, and add the second only when you have a concrete second workload (e.g., GPU experiments).

Key takeaways

  • Get domain + business email + company website + entity in place before applying — most rejections are identity problems.
  • Start with self-serve tiers: AWS Activate Founders ($1K, up to $5K), Microsoft for Startups (unlock toward $150K), Google Start (up to $2K).
  • Partner paths unlock larger pools: AWS Portfolio up to $200K with Org ID; Google Scale up to $200K / $350K AI-first for VC-backed stages.
  • Stack programs carefully, set 50/75/90% billing alerts, and verify live terms — blog numbers go stale.
  • Credits buy validation time; they do not replace architecture judgment or cost discipline.
