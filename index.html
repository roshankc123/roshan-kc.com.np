About me

Right after my bachelor’s degree, I started teaching. On the side, Bibek and I took freelance projects from clients. One of those clients got funding and asked us to register a company — so we officially registered MarginTop Solutions (opens in a new tab). A few months later, Samir joined too. By the end of that same year, we moved to Kathmandu.

Where the name comes from

MarginTop Solutions was a name we decided in our first year of college. We were in a lot of programs and competitions, and we already knew we would start a company someday. We brainstormed a few names, landed on MarginTop, and after that we used the same brand in every program and hackathon we joined.

Growing the team

For the first six months we worked completely online. Then came the growth phase. As our client base expanded and project complexity grew, we knew we were onto something bigger. We hired more people — we even hired a maid and started providing khaja in the office. Personally, I felt the pain of growth with no prior experience. We had to work on everything. As CTO, I was DevOps at night, backend developer in the evening, Flutter during the day — and team management sat on top of all of it.

MarginTop today

We primarily relied on a third person to get projects. We soon realized that was not the right way, and now we have started approaching clients directly. I am learning marketing, social media management, ads, and video editing — and I think that is the best gift of starting early: there is always energy to learn. Today MarginTop is growing slowly and stably. Our team size is 16 and still growing. In parallel, we are working on products of our own.

Top domains I’ve worked in

Robotics: control software for autonomous mobile robots

The software operators use to map, program and run industrial robots, both from a browser and from a desktop app that talks to the controller directly.

  • A web control app on ROS 2 (via rosbridge) with live map rendering, mapping and navigation modes, routes, waypoints, restricted and special zones, joystick teleop and run statistics.
  • Visual programming with Blockly and custom robot blocks, so operators can build missions without writing code.
  • Leading the codebase's move to Domain-Driven Design and Clean Architecture, one module at a time (strangler pattern), with JavaScript migrated to TypeScript.
  • An Electron desktop configurator that talks to the controller over USB serial. It uses custom ZMODEM / XMODEM file transfer, YAML config packages, i18n and auto-update.
  • A Flutter provisioning app plus Ubuntu-side Python services (hotspot creator, Wi-Fi scanner API) to put robots on site networks.

Mobile apps that pair with a Bluetooth-mesh therapy device, run treatment sessions and keep clinical data safe even when the phone is offline.

  • Long-running background sessions and local notifications that survive the OS killing the app.
  • Offline-first sync with self-hosted Supabase + PowerSync on Docker, plus a Laravel / React admin dashboard for clinicians.
